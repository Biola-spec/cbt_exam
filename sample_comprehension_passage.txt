The Industrial Revolution, which began in Britain in the late 18th century, was a period of major industrialization that transformed largely rural, agrarian societies into industrial and urban ones. This transformation had profound effects on socioeconomic and cultural conditions, starting in Great Britain and later spreading to Western Europe, North America, and eventually the rest of the world.

The First Industrial Revolution, which began around 1760 and lasted until about 1840, was characterized by the mechanization of textile production, the development of iron-making techniques, and the increased use of steam power. The Second Industrial Revolution, from about 1870 to 1914, was marked by steel production, mass production techniques, and the widespread use of electricity.

Key inventions of this period included the steam engine, which powered machinery and locomotives, the spinning jenny and power loom for textile production, and the telegraph for communication. These innovations led to the growth of factories, urbanization, and significant changes in labor practices.

The Industrial Revolution brought both benefits and challenges. While it led to increased production, improved transportation, and higher standards of living for some, it also resulted in poor working conditions, child labor, and environmental degradation. These issues eventually led to labor reforms and the development of labor unions.
